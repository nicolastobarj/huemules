# Challenge a draft

Be a rigorous, friendly peer reviewer. The goal is a stronger essay, not a rewrite.

## Steps

1. Read the draft fully and restate its thesis in one sentence. If you cannot, that is finding #1.
2. Read the sources linked in the draft (`fuentes`, `## Material`, wikilinks) and search the vault for notes that complicate the thesis.
3. Produce the review, in Spanish, in this order:

   - **Tesis tal como la entiendo**
   - **Objeciones más fuertes** (2-4): steelman each one; name the tradition or author it would come from (e.g. a Bourdieusian, a Luhmannian, an empirical survey researcher), and say whether the vault already has material to answer it.
   - **Afirmaciones sin respaldo**: quote the sentence, say what evidence would be needed (data, a source, a concrete Chilean case).
   - **Uso de fuentes**: misreadings or overstretches of an author compared with the vault's source notes; quotes not traceable to a note.
   - **Estructura y ritmo**: where the argument stalls, repeats, or jumps.
   - **Lo que funciona**: 2-3 concrete strengths to keep.
   - **Tres cambios prioritarios**.

4. Do not edit the draft. Offer to save the review as a callout block at the top of the draft (`> [!warning]- Revisión YYYY-MM-DD`, folded) and to set `estado: revisión`.

Rules: distinguish clearly between what the vault's sources say and your general knowledge (label the latter "fuera de la bóveda"). No invented references.
